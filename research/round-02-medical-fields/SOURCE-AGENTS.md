> Research mission (round 2, medical content): for an open-source (AGPL-3.0) medical simulation system — 3D anatomy atlas, physiology engine, imaging suite, surgical procedure simulator, and clinical-reasoning workbench — establish the authoritative, quantitative state of knowledge across the medical fields the system must model. For every resource: exact name, what authoritative content it provides (with headline numbers/ranges where that is the point), who publishes it, openness/licensing for use in an open-source educational product, and source URL. Cover ALL fronts:
> 
> 1. ANATOMY SCIENCE: Bergman's Comprehensive Encyclopedia of Human Anatomic Variation (scope, access), major regional/surgical anatomy references and atlases used in surgical training, cross-sectional anatomy standards, embryology/development references relevant to congenital variants, and any large published datasets of anatomical measurements (organ sizes, vessel diameters) with normal ranges.
> 
> 2. QUANTITATIVE PHYSIOLOGY & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY: the standard quantitative references for cardiovascular/respiratory/renal parameters (Guyton & Hall lineage and similar), published normal ranges for hemodynamics (pressures, volumes, resistances), lung mechanics, renal clearances; shock classification numbers (ATLS hemorrhage classes), sepsis definitions and bundles (Surviving Sepsis Campaign), acid-base interpretation standards.
> 
> 3. SURGERY & PERIOPERATIVE STANDARDS: WHO Surgical Safety Checklist evidence, ERAS Society protocols, ATLS (Advanced Trauma Life Support) current edition standards, damage-control surgery principles, ASA anesthesia standards and monitoring requirements, antibiotic prophylaxis standards (ASHP/IDSA), and current evidence summaries for foundational procedures (laparoscopic cholecystectomy safety/Critical View of Safety literature, appendectomy, hernia repair) — what a simulator must teach to be current.
> 
> 4. PHARMACOLOGY (quantitative): open or citable sources for drug data — OpenFDA, WHO ATC/DDD system, DrugBank licensing status, published anesthetic pharmacology parameter sets (MAC values for volatile agents, propofol Marsh/Schnider TCI model parameters, opioid context-sensitive half-times), pressor dosing ranges, and any openly published PK/PD model libraries.
> 
> 5. IMAGING SCIENCE: CT Hounsfield unit reference values by tissue (published tables), ACR appropriateness criteria openness, ultrasound/FAST exam evidence base and standards, MRI contrast basics from authoritative open sources, radiology teaching-file resources and their licenses (e.g., Radiopaedia terms of use — flag restrictions precisely).
> 
> 6. DIAGNOSTIC REASONING EVIDENCE: the JAMA Rational Clinical Examination series (what it provides: likelihood ratios for exam findings), published sensitivity/specificity compendia, diagnostic error research (National Academy of Medicine report findings), cognitive bias/debiasing literature, pretest-probability resources, and calibration-training evidence.
> 
> 7. DISEASE & EPIDEMIOLOGY DATA: Global Burden of Disease (IHME) data openness and scope, CDC WONDER, SEER cancer statistics access, Orphanet rare-disease data licensing, and disease natural-history references suitable for building simulated cases with realistic base rates.
> 
> 8. MEDICAL EDUCATION SCIENCE: USMLE content outlines (what a physician must know — as a coverage map), AAMC curriculum references, simulation-based mastery learning evidence (McGaghie et al.), OSCE/assessment standards, spaced-repetition evidence in medical education, and competency frameworks (ACGME milestones, EPAs) relevant to scoring a surgical trainee.
> 
> 9. SPECIAL POPULATIONS & VARIATION: pediatric physiology/anatomy differences and published scaling (Broselow system, pediatric normal ranges), obstetric physiology changes in pregnancy (published values), geriatric changes, sex-based differences in anatomy/physiology with authoritative sources, and body-habitus variation — the data needed to simulate patients beyond one reference adult.
> 
> 10. FRONTIER CLINICAL TECHNOLOGY a state-of-the-art system should anticipate: robotic surgery platforms and their training ecosystems (Intuitive da Vinci and new entrants), surgical data science (e.g., OR black box research), intraoperative navigation/AR guidance evidence, FDA-cleared AI in radiology/surgery landscape at a high level, and telementoring/telesurgery status.
> 
> Deliverable shape: structured report by the ten fronts, each item name / authoritative content (with key numbers) / openness verdict / URL; end with (a) the coverage map — which medical fields now have citable open foundations and which remain paywalled or expert-knowledge-only, and (b) the ten highest-value sources to build on first.
