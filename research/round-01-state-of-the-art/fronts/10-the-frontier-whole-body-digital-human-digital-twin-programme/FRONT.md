**Honest paragraph — where the frontier's ceiling actually sits, and what is reachable.** After roughly three decades, >$350M of SPARC money, €100M+ of European VHT money, and a decade of Dassault's commercial effort, the frontier's *demonstrated* ceiling is: **one organ modelled to regulatory credibility (the Living Heart), several organs modelled to research credibility, excellent standards and repositories for exchanging models (CellML/SBML/FieldML/SED-ML, PMR, SPARC Portal), and zero — anywhere, at any budget — integrated, validated, whole-body, cell-to-organ digital humans.** Every serious programme quietly re-scoped from “the virtual human” to patient-specific or problem-specific models and virtual cohorts; EDITH, the most recent flagship, was funded to write the roadmap for the integrated twin, not to build it. The binding constraints are not rendering or compute: they are (1) validation data (you cannot validate what you cannot measure in living humans at scale), (2) parameter identifiability in multiscale coupling, and (3) the sheer curation labour of keeping thousands of models mutually consistent. **Reachable by a small OSS team within a few years, and genuinely frontier-adjacent:** a standards-native platform that (a) hosts a provenance-tracked canonical body (Front 1), (b) runs a validated lumped physiology engine (Pulse) *and* imports CellML/SBML models from PMR/SPARC so new organ models slot in without code, (c) does one or two organs at high fidelity (FEM heart/liver in SOFA) with Living-Heart-style published validation dossiers, (d) exposes virtual-cohort variation (Front 11 variants) rather than one “normal” human, and (e) publishes its validity evidence openly — something no commercial incumbent does. That is not the whole frontier, but it is the part of the frontier that is actually unoccupied.
