### 3. Emergency & Resuscitation

**AHA ACLS / BLS — 2020 AHA Guidelines for CPR & ECC (*Circulation* 2020; still the cited base with focused updates).**
Numbers (adult): compressions **100–120/min**, depth **≥5 cm, ≤6 cm (2–2.4 in)**, full recoil, interruptions **<10 s**, rotate compressors q2 min; ratio 30:2 without advanced airway, continuous + 1 breath/6 s with. Defibrillation VF/pVT: **biphasic 120–200 J** (manufacturer dose; escalate same/higher), **monophasic 360 J**. Epinephrine **1 mg IV/IO q3–5 min** — *as early as feasible in non-shockable* rhythms; in shockable rhythms after initial defibrillation attempts fail. Amiodarone **300 mg** (2nd dose 150 mg) or lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg for shock-refractory VF/pVT. Synchronised cardioversion reference points: AF 120–200 J biphasic, SVT/flutter 50–100 J.
**PALS (2020):** rate 100–120/min; depth **≥⅓ AP diameter** (≈5 cm child, ≈4 cm infant); 2-rescuer ratio **15:2**; defibrillation **2 J/kg → 4 J/kg → ≥4 up to max 10 J/kg** (or adult dose); epinephrine **0.01 mg/kg IV/IO q3–5 min** (max 1 mg); amiodarone **5 mg/kg**; synchronised cardioversion **0.5–1 J/kg** (then 2 J/kg).
Openness: **CITE-ONLY, strictly.** AHA guideline *articles* are readable in *Circulation*; AHA course books, algorithm posters/cards and eLearning are **commercial products** — never ship scans/redrawn copies of AHA algorithms. Re-draw original flowcharts encoding the same numbers, labelled “consistent with AHA 2020” with citation. URLs (secondary summaries agreeing on all numbers above): https://deepmedicine.in/clinical/protocols/acls-algorithm.html ; https://www.statpearls.com/ArticleLibrary/viewarticle/18926 — *index*.

**Stroke — NIHSS + AHA/ASA Early Management 2019 (Powers et al.).**
Numbers: **NIHSS: 15 items, 0–42** (0 none; 1–4 minor; 5–15 moderate; 16–20 moderate-severe; 21–42 severe; ≥4-point change = clinically significant). IV thrombolysis (alteplase; tenecteplase increasingly used): **within 4.5 h** of last-known-well in eligible patients (BP <185/110, glucose, anticoagulant, and imaging exclusions apply — encode the full checklist, not the window alone). Thrombectomy: **NIHSS ≥6 + ASPECTS ≥6 + ICA/M1 occlusion within 6 h** standard; **up to 24 h** in DAWN/DEFUSE-3-selected patients (clinical/imaging mismatch, small core/large penumbra); basilar: NIHSS ≥10 within 24 h in trial-selected patients. System targets to teach alongside: door-to-needle ≤60 min, door-to-imaging ≤25 min (standard stroke-centre metrics).
Openness: **NIHSS itself: BUILD** (US-government scale, in the public domain in practice; training/certification programmes around it are commercial — ship the scale, not a certification course). **AHA/ASA guideline text: CITE-ONLY.** URL: https://www.sismed-it.com/en/nihss-stroke-scale-calculator/ — *index*.

**STEMI — ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI ACS Guideline 2025 (replaces 2013 STEMI guideline).**
Numbers: ECG **≤10 min** from first medical contact (FMC). Primary PCI: **FMC/door-to-device ≤90 min** (direct presenters); transfer: **FMC-to-device ≤120 min** and **door-in–door-out ≤30 min** at the non-PCI hospital; if PCI cannot meet 120 min → **fibrinolysis door-to-needle ≤30 min**, then transfer (pharmaco-invasive/rescue PCI 3–24 h if failed). Radial access preferred; complete revascularisation of non-culprit lesions in stable multivessel STEMI upgraded to Class I in 2025.
Openness: **CITE-ONLY** (ACC/AHA *JACC/Circulation*). Numbers encodable. URL: https://sattimd.com/guidelines/acc-acs-2025.html — *index*.

**Tranexamic acid — CRASH-2 (*Lancet* 2010) & WOMAN (*Lancet* 2017), LSHTM-led.**
Numbers: CRASH-2: n=**20,211**, 274 hospitals/40 countries, TXA **1 g IV over 10 min + 1 g over 8 h**, randomised within 8 h. All-cause mortality **14.5% vs 16.0% (RR 0.91; NNT ≈67)**; death from bleeding 4.9% vs 5.7% (RR 0.85). Time interaction is the teaching point: benefit concentrated **≤3 h** (≈⅓ reduction in bleeding death ≤1 h); **>3 h: no benefit / possible harm — do not give late.** WOMAN: n=**20,060**, 193 hospitals/21 countries, TXA **1 g IV** (± second 1 g) for postpartum haemorrhage: death from bleeding **1.5% vs 1.9% (RR 0.81)**; **within 3 h of birth RR 0.69**; hysterectomy *not* reduced (RR 1.02).
Openness: **CITE-ONLY for Lancet texts; numbers BUILD.** (CRASH trials' educational summaries from LSHTM are freely shared; still cite, don't copy.) URLs: https://coreem.net/journal-reviews/crash-2-tranexamic-acid-in-major-trauma/ ; https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(17)30638-4/fulltext — *index*.

**TCCC vs civilian care — Committee on TCCC / Joint Trauma System (guidelines continuously updated; current version via Deployed Medicine/JTS).**
Differences to encode as *contrasting rule-sets*, not one “trauma mode”: phases **Care Under Fire → Tactical Field Care → Tactical Evacuation Care** (no civilian analogue); **MARCH** priority with immediate high-and-tight limb tourniquets + haemostatic dressings *before* airway (civilian ATLS historically airway-first, though civilian practice has converged toward catastrophic-haemorrhage-first/C-ABC — teach the convergence honestly); permissive hypotension / whole-blood-forward resuscitation, TXA + calcium early, ketamine-forward analgesia, early broad-spectrum antibiotics for penetrating wounds, minimal crystalloid, aggressive hypothermia prevention, prolonged-field-care expectations (hours–days) vs civilian short transport + ATLS/definitive-care timelines; tourniquet *conversion/reposition* ideally **<2 h**, do not convert after **>6 h** in the field per current TCCC teaching. Note: a May 2026 TCCC revision was reported in secondary sources this round (expanded conversion to lower tiers, analgesia/antibiotic changes) — **flagged as index-only, single-source; verify against the current JTS guideline PDF before encoding 2026 specifics.**
Openness: **BUILD-leaning** — TCCC guidelines are US-military public works, distributed free (Deployed Medicine app/site); cite JTS/CoTCCC and date-stamp the version encoded, because TCCC changes faster than any civilian guideline. URL: https://tccc.org.ua/en/guide/introduction-to-tccc (mirror of TCCC intro curriculum) — *index*.

**Toxicology antidotes — TOXBASE / UK National Poisons Information Service (NPIS).**
Content: ~21,000 product entries; the UK first-line clinical toxicology database (antidotes, antivenoms, pregnancy, chemicals). Access: **free only to NHS/UKHSA/MOD/ac.uk registrants**; departments/overseas by **annual subscription**; app free for eligible users, £6.99/yr limited version for others; **not available for public access**. US analogue (PoisonHelp / regional poison centres) is phone-service, not an open database; EXTOXNET/HSDB remnants and LITFL/EMCrit toxicology libraries are the open teaching layer.
Openness: **CLOSED for TOXBASE content — cite-only, never scrape or embed monographs.** Build the antidote table (agent → antidote → dose) from primary drug labelling/open references (e.g. NAC 150+50+100 mg/kg 21-h regimen, naloxone, sodium bicarbonate for TCA with QRS >100 ms — as surfaced in open EM summaries) with “check TOXBASE/poison centre” behaviour taught in-case. URLs: https://www.toxbase.org/information-for-toxbase-subscribers/ ; https://www.npis.org/Toxbase.html — *index*.
