### 5. Tissue Biomechanics & Surgical Physics

> **Honesty note (must ship with the engine):** there is no single “modulus of liver.” Published values for the same tissue routinely span **an order of magnitude** with method (indentation vs rheometry vs elastography), frequency/strain, perfusion, temperature, species, and disease. Shear modulus G and Young's modulus E differ by ~3× for incompressible tissue (E ≈ 3G) and are routinely conflated in secondary tables. The engine should store **ranges + method tags**, expose the range in teacher view, and tune *feel/relative ordering* (brain ≪ liver < muscle ≪ cartilage ≪ bone), not pretend to Pa-level truth.

**Elastic moduli — indicative Young's modulus E ranges (compiled mechanobiology literature).**
Brain **0.1–1 kPa** (some rheometry 1–3 kPa); liver **0.3–6 kPa** (healthy elastography ~<5–6.5 kPa; fibrotic 3–22 kPa shear-derived; cirrhosis >12 kPa, severe disease >40 kPa reported); lung 0.2–2 kPa (fibrotic ~17 kPa); kidney 4–8 kPa; vessel wall **1–10 kPa at low strain** (layered/anisotropic; MPa-range at high/physiological tensile strain — the widest method trap in the table); skeletal muscle **8–17 kPa** passive; skin **MPa range** (abdominal epidermis ~6 MPa young vs >10 MPa older/drier — skin must not be modelled with kPa soft-tissue defaults); cartilage bulk **25 kPa–>1 MPa** (pericellular matrix far softer); ligament **25–93 MPa**; osteoid 25–40 kPa; **calcified bone 1–20 GPa** (~6 orders of magnitude above brain).

**Suture properties — retention, not just breaking strength, is the simulatable variable.**
Numbers (tensile-strength retention / absorption): Vicryl (polyglactin 910, braided): **75% @2 wk, 50% @3 wk, 25% @4 wk**; absorbed 56–70 d. Monocryl (poliglecaprone, mono): ~**50–60% @1 wk, 20–30% @2 wk**, gone by ~3 wk; absorbed 91–119 d. PDS II (polydioxanone, mono): **70% @2 wk, 50% @4 wk, 25% @6 wk**; absorbed 180–210 d — the prolonged-support choice (fascia). Plain gut 7–10 d strength; chromic gut 21–28 d. Silk: non-absorbable but loses ~50% by 1 yr, high tissue reactivity. Nylon: ~20% loss/yr. Polypropylene/steel: indefinite. Handling rules to encode: monofilament = low drag/low infection harbouring, more throws needed, memory; braided = better knot security/handling, more drag/reactivity; standard teaching = 4 throws for most synthetics in the cited university tables (knot security is material-specific — do not universalise).

**Electrosurgery physics — SAGES Fundamentals of Laparoscopic Surgery (FLS) canon.**
Numbers/logic: ESU converts mains to high-frequency RF. **Cut:** continuous, *unmodulated, low-voltage* waveform → rapid heating, intracellular water → steam, cell vaporisation; minimal lateral damage, poor haemostasis; electrode hovers (no direct contact). **Coag:** *intermittent, damped, high-peak-voltage* waveform (low duty cycle) → slower heating, desiccation/fulguration, shallow eschar, better haemostasis, more spread. **Blend:** alternating cut/coag duty cycles. Protein denaturation/coagulation begins **>60 °C**; vaporisation at **100 °C**. Current density ∝ power, ∝ 1/area² — small tip = cutting density, large dispersive pad = safe return (pad off hair/bony prominence/scar, over vascular muscle). Laparoscopic hazards to simulate: insulation failure, capacitive coupling, direct coupling. Bipolar: current only between jaws, lower energy, seals vessels **up to 7 mm** (advanced bipolar, impedance-feedback). Ultrasonic: **~55 kHz** (FLS teaching: 50,000 Hz class) mechanical vibration, no patient current, blade temps to ~105 °C.
**Thermal spread (measured, porcine/human literature):** histologic depth ~**0.4–1.2 mm** in head-to-head porcine work at 30 W (monopolar ovary 0.99±0.82 mm; bipolar uterus 1.15±0.2 mm; ultrasonic uterus 0.48±0.23 mm; plasma lowest) — while *lateral* spread across human-tissue studies spans **0–7.64 mm** by device/tissue/method (2026 systematic review: no reliable device ranking possible). Monopolar tissue >42 °C detectable **1 cm** from the tip in cited porcine work. Engine rule: model spread in **single-digit mm**, monopolar > bipolar ≈ ultrasonic on average, with wide variance bands — any product claiming “device X spreads exactly Y mm” is overstating the literature.

**Laparoscopic insufflation — pressures & physiology.**
Numbers: CO₂ (non-flammable, soluble — embolism-tolerant relative to air), working pressure **12–15 mmHg** adults (FLS max preset **15 mmHg**; total volume typically 1–3 L at establishment in teaching texts); low-pressure surgery 6–8 mmHg studied; paediatric lower (infants ~8 mmHg; 10–12 mmHg older children in cited teaching). Physiology the engine must couple: at ~12 mmHg ↑CVP, ↑SVR (to ~+65%), ↑PVR (to ~+90% in cited anaesthesia reviews); **≥15 mmHg IVC compression → ↓venous return → ↓cardiac output (to −50% in reverse-Trendelenburg/low-reserve patients)**; diaphragm splinting → ↓FRC/compliance, ↑peak airway pressure, V/Q mismatch; peritoneal CO₂ absorption → hypercapnia/respiratory acidosis unless minute ventilation ↑; renal blood flow ↓35–60%, oliguria; vagal bradycardia on rapid insufflation. Normal resting IAP <5 mmHg for contrast.
