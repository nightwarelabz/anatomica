### 9. Global & Resource-Tiered Medicine

**Lancet Commission on Global Surgery — *Global Surgery 2030* (*Lancet* 2015, Meara et al.).**
Headline numbers (the citable lineage): **5 billion people** lack access to safe, affordable surgical/anaesthesia care when needed; **143 million additional procedures/year** needed in LMICs; **16.9 million deaths/year** from conditions requiring surgical care (2010 baseline, ~⅓ of all deaths); **33 million people/year** face catastrophic expenditure from surgery costs (81 million including non-medical costs in later framings); only **6%** of procedures occur in the poorest third of the world; scale-up cost **~$420 billion to 2030** vs **$12.3 trillion** in lost LMIC output without it. Six indicators/targets for 2030: **80%** timely essential-surgery coverage; **≥20 SAO** (surgeon–anaesthetist–obstetrician) physicians **/100,000**; **5,000 procedures/100,000/year**; tracked perioperative mortality; 100% protection against impoverishing and catastrophic expenditure. Bellwether procedures: **caesarean delivery, laparotomy, open-fracture treatment**.
Openness: **CITE-ONLY for the *Lancet* report text; headline indicators BUILD as cited facts** (they are WHO/World Bank-adopted indicators). URLs: http://www.harvardmagazine.com/2015/06/surgery-for-all ; https://www.lse.ac.uk/lse-health/events/2022-23/surgical-outcomes/ — *index*.

**WHO Essential Surgery / DCP3 — World Bank, *Disease Control Priorities*, 3rd ed., Essential Surgery volume (2015).**
Content: **44 essential surgical procedures** judged cost-effective/feasible at district-hospital level, organised by platform (first-level hospital, etc.); WHA Resolution **68.15 (2015)** commits member states to emergency/essential surgical care within UHC. DCP3 surgical-volume benchmark aligns with the Commission's **5,000/100,000** (current LMIC reality is often an order of magnitude lower — e.g. Sierra Leone ~1,520/100,000 by population survey; rural Nepal districts 387/100,000 total in cited work).
Openness: **BUILD-leaning — DCP3 volumes are published open-access (CC BY) via World Bank/NCBI Bookshelf in practice; WHO guidance is freely available for educational use with attribution.** Verify the licence line on the specific DCP3 chapter PDF before mirroring any table. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12435603/ — *index*.

**ASCO Resource-Stratified Guidelines — American Society of Clinical Oncology (framework since 2016).**
Model (the transferable design pattern, beyond oncology): every recommendation is issued at **four tiers — Basic** (core, single-interaction, must-function), **Limited** (second-tier, major outcome gains, modest infrastructure), **Enhanced** (third-tier, more options/choice), **Maximal** (state-of-the-art, depends on all lower tiers existing). Worked example (cervical-cancer secondary prevention update): HPV-DNA testing all tiers, VIA acceptable in Basic; screening maximal 25–65 q5y vs basic 30–49, 1–3× per lifetime.
Openness: **CITE-ONLY** (ASCO/*JCO Global Oncology*). The *four-tier pattern itself* is the reusable idea for Anatomica's resource modes. URL: https://ascopubs.org/doi/pdfdirect/10.1200/GO.22.00217 — *index*.

*What low-resource design changes in the teaching system (synthesis from the three sources above):* (1) every procedure wave needs an **equipment-tier variant** (no piped O₂ / no ventilator / no blood bank / no CT branches — e.g. torsion and AAA decisions without Doppler/CT, ketamine-based anaesthesia, non-operative options where evidence supports them); (2) teach **Bellwether-first** prioritisation and task-sharing/generalist-team realities; (3) time/distance and cost-to-patient must be modelled variables (a “correct” referral the patient cannot afford/reach is a failed plan — catastrophic-expenditure framing); (4) improvisation must be taught as *standards with evidence* (e.g. WHO Surgical Safety Checklist — free WHO material — works without any capital equipment), never as “anything goes.”
