### 8. Human Factors, Safety & Debriefing Science

**OR Crew Resource Management (CRM) — aviation-derived; VA Medical Team Training lineage.**
Evidence (teach effect sizes with their study designs): VA medical team training across >70 hospitals was associated with a **~50% greater decline / relative mortality advantage** in trained vs untrained facilities in the commonly cited programme evaluations (Neily et al., *JAMA* lineage — observational, not RCT); WHO Surgical Safety Checklist implementation (Haynes et al.) associated with **~40% fewer complications** in the original multi-country study. CRM content spine: briefs/huddles/debriefs, checklists, flattened hierarchy/speak-up, workload distribution, situational awareness. Present all CRM outcome numbers as *association-level* evidence — overclaiming causality is the classic error here.
Openness: **BUILD concepts** (CRM is a body of practice, not a copyrighted text); specific curricula vary (see TeamSTEPPS). URL: https://clinicalpub.com/?p=62634 — *index*.

**TeamSTEPPS 3.0 + SBAR — AHRQ (US Dept. of Health & Human Services) with DoD.**
Content: Four skills — **Communication, Team Leadership, Situation Monitoring, Mutual Support**. Tools: **SBAR** (Situation–Background–Assessment–Recommendation; Kaiser Permanente origin, disseminated via IHI/AHRQ), call-out, check-back (closed loop), handoff (I-PASS-adjacent), brief/huddle/debrief, **CUS** (“I'm Concerned, I'm Uncomfortable, this is a Safety issue”) + two-challenge rule. AHRQ provides free online modules, Pocket Guide, and full curricula.
Openness: **BUILD — AHRQ materials are US-government works, free to use/adapt with attribution.** This is the single most reusable asset in this section: the workbench's teamwork scoring can be built directly on TeamSTEPPS tools. URL: https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/intro/teach/two-day.html — *index*.

**Reason's error model — James Reason, *Human Error* (1990) / *Managing the Risks of Organizational Accidents* (1997).**
Lineage/content: person approach (“name, blame, shame”) vs **system approach**; **active failures** (sharp-end slips/lapses/mistakes/violations) vs **latent conditions** (design, supervision, training, staffing, unworkable procedures — dormant until combined with local triggers); **Swiss-cheese model**: harm occurs only when holes in successive defensive layers align. In-case design use: every adverse-event case should be debriefable at *both* levels (what the clinician did; what the system set up) — a case that only blames the sharp end is pedagogically wrong under Reason.
Openness: **BUILD the concepts/diagrams in original form** (books are Cambridge/Ashgate copyright — don't copy Reason's figures). URL: https://performanceandimprovement.nhs.wales/functions/quality-safety-and-improvement/improvement/improvement-cymru-academy/resource-library/academy-toolkit-guides/reasons-swiss-cheese-safety-model-toolkit/ — *index*.

**PEARLS debriefing — Eppich & Cheng, *Simulation in Healthcare* 2015.**
Content: **P**romoting **E**xcellence **a**nd **R**eflective **L**earning in **S**imulation. Phases: **Setting the Scene → Reactions → Description → Analysis → Summary** (often counted as 4 core phases + scene-setting). Analysis blends three strategies chosen by gap type/time: **learner self-assessment (plus–delta)**, **focused facilitation (incl. advocacy–inquiry)**, **directive feedback/teaching**. Scripted prompts exist in the PEARLS Healthcare Debriefing Tool (Bajaj et al. 2018).
Openness: **BUILD the framework into the debrief engine** (frameworks/methods are teachable; the journal article + official tool sheets are copyright — implement in original wording, cite Eppich & Cheng). URL: https://www.healthysimulation.com/pearls-debriefing/ — *index*.

**Assessment psychometrics — Messick's unified validity (1989) / AERA-APA-NCME *Standards* (2014).**
Reporting standard to adopt for every scored simulation: validity is a property of the *interpretation/use* of scores, not of the tool. Report evidence under **5 sources: (1) content, (2) response process, (3) internal structure, (4) relations to other variables, (5) consequences**. Reliability is necessary but insufficient: report inter-rater reliability as **ICC** (exemplar sim-tool study: inter-rater ICC 0.887, intra-rater 0.91, with Delphi content consensus 80% and known-groups separation by training level — a realistic quality bar), internal consistency (Cronbach's α) where checklists are summed, plus standard-setting method for any pass/fail cut. A score shipped without this argument must be labelled *formative/unvalidated*.
Openness: **BUILD the reporting practice** (framework is standard methodology). URLs: https://www.healthysimulation.com/healthcare-simulation-tool-validity/ ; https://ssh.expoplanner.com/files/12/SpeakerFiles/27133_Handout_IMSH_2021_Validity_of_Assessment_Presentation_Slides.pdf — *index*.

**Stress inoculation in surgical training — Meichenbaum SIT (1985), adapted to simulation.**
Content: 3 phases: **(1) conceptualisation** (how stress degrades calculation, recall, technical skill), **(2) skills acquisition/rehearsal** (coping, cognitive restructuring, task-oriented coping), **(3) graduated application** under progressively realistic simulated stress. Evidence posture (honest): stress reliably *impairs* resuscitation/technical performance in studies; SIT/mental-training packages improve coping and team performance in small surgical studies (e.g. Wetzel carotid-crisis simulation: stress-management training → better OTAS teamwork scores, lower stress); brief one-off exposure alone shows weak objective-performance effects — benefits are **dose-dependent and skills-mediated**. Design implication: graduated stressors + explicit coping teaching + debrief, never “stress for realism” alone.
Openness: **BUILD** (method + open literature). URL: https://ctv.veeva.com/study/simulation-based-stress-inoculation-training — *index*.
