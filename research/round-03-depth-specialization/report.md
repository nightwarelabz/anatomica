# Anatomica — Round 3: Depth & Specialization — Authoritative Quantitative Foundations

**System:** open-source (AGPL-3.0) medical simulation — 3D anatomy atlas, physiology engine, imaging suite, CTA-authored surgical simulator, clinical-reasoning workbench.
**Date researched:** 2026-11-05 (America/Chicago). **Method:** web-index research throughout; every fact below is flagged *index*. No claim in this round was verified in the live browser window — treat guideline *numbers* as high-confidence where two independent summaries agreed, and licence *verdicts* as careful readings of publisher/policy pages, not legal advice. **How to read openness:** **BUILD** = may embed data/text/numbers in the AGPL product (with attribution where required); **CITE-ONLY** = may teach the facts/numbers and link/cite, but may not copy text, figures, algorithms-as-images, or redistribute data; **CLOSED** = paywalled/subscription/credentialed content that cannot be shipped or scraped.

## Summary

The deep layer is buildable, but on a different basis than Rounds 1–2: the *numbers* of modern medicine (thresholds, doses, windows, pressures) are almost all facts that can be taught and encoded, while the *texts, charts and databases* that carry them are mostly cite-only or credentialed. Three hard boundaries dominate. (1) **Patient data:** only truly open sets (VitalDB CC BY 4.0, most PhysioNet Open Access sets, most TCIA CC BY collections) can ship with or train a distributed product; MIMIC-IV/eICU/HiRID/AmsterdamUMCdb are credentialed — usable by a credentialed developer for validation, never redistributable, so the engine must ship with synthetic/open data and treat MIMIC as an off-line validation harness. (2) **Guideline prose:** ASA, AHA, ASRA, IDSA, AABB, CLSI content is copyrighted even when PDFs are readable — encode the numbers as original logic, cite, never paste algorithms/figures. The big exceptions are US-government works (AHRQ TeamSTEPPS, CDC, WHO material, FLS-style facts via textbooks) which are genuinely reusable. (3) **Regulation:** a purely educational simulator with no patient-specific recommendation on real patients stays outside FDA device CDS and EU MDR device scope; the moment it processes a real patient's image/signal or directs a real patient's care it crosses Criterion 1 / Rule 11 into device territory (EU: at least Class IIa). Keep the educational intended-use statement, disclaimers, and “no real-patient use” boundary as load-bearing product features, not boilerplate.

## Findings

### 1. Open Clinical Data & Signals

**PhysioNet (Research Resource for Complex Physiologic Signals) — MIT Laboratory for Computational Physiology / NIH.**
Content: Three access tiers govern everything: *Open Access* (anyone, minimal restrictions), *Restricted Access* (registered + Data Use Agreement), *Credentialed Access* (credentialing + human-subjects training + DUA). Open waveform holdings directly relevant to a monitoring engine include: MIT-BIH Arrhythmia and related BIDMC sets; Apnea-ECG; BIDMC PPG and Respiration Dataset; a large-scale 12-lead ECG database for arrhythmia study (>10,000 patients); BUT PPG (3,888 × 10-s smartphone PPG records with ECG); MIMIC-III Waveform Database — continuous ECG, PPG, ABP and other signals + periodic measurements from ~30,000 ICU patients (listed as Open Access on the database index — note the *clinical* MIMIC-III/IV databases are credentialed; do not conflate waveform vs clinical access on a given version; check the per-project licence page before use). Licences in use across PhysioNet include CC BY 4.0, Open Data Commons licences, and PhysioNet Restricted/Credentialed Health Data License 1.5.0 — licence is *per-project*, not site-wide.
Openness: **BUILD for Open Access projects under their stated CC/ODC licence (attribute, cite DOI); CITE-ONLY / NO-SHIP for Restricted; CLOSED-TO-REDISTRIBUTION for Credentialed.** URL: https://physionet.org/about/database/ — *index*.

**VitalDB (Vital Signs DataBase) — Seoul National University Hospital; Lee et al., *Scientific Data* 2022.**
Content: 6,388 non-cardiac surgical cases; 486,451 waveform/numeric tracks; 196 intraoperative monitoring parameters + 73 perioperative clinical + 34 lab parameters; 12 waveform types incl. ECG (500 Hz via SNUADC analogue capture), invasive arterial pressure, plethysmogram/PPG, EEG-derived indices; the single best open source for an *anaesthesia/intraoperative* monitoring engine — arterial-line, ECG and PPG behaviour under real anaesthesia.
Openness: **BUILD — CC BY 4.0, Open Access on PhysioNet, no DUA, no credentialing** (PhysioNet v1.0.0 statement). This is the flagship shippable training/validation set for the theatre/monitoring engine. URL: https://vitaldb.metrology-atlas.org/data-and-licence.html — *index*.

**MIMIC-IV — MIT-LCP / Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (2008–2019, Boston).**
Content: De-identified EHR for ICU/hospital care: demographics, labs, vitals/chart events, meds, procedures, respiratory support, diagnoses; companion projects MIMIC-IV-ECG, MIMIC-CXR, MIMIC-IV Waveform derivatives. Access steps: (1) become PhysioNet-credentialed (CITI “Data or Specimens Only Research” training), (2) sign the DUA, (3) download or cloud (BigQuery/GCS). Licence: PhysioNet Credentialed Health Data License 1.5.0 — no re-identification, no sharing access, research-only use, must contribute code from publications to an open repository. Derived files **cannot be redistributed** — anyone reproducing work must rebuild from their own credentialed access.
Openness: **CLOSED-TO-REDISTRIBUTION; legitimate as a developer-side validation/tuning harness only.** Design rule: no MIMIC rows, waveforms or derived tables in the repo, installer, or “demo data.” Ship open/synthetic data; document MIMIC validation as a reproducible script a credentialed user runs locally. URLs: https://mimic.mit.edu/docs/gettingstarted/ ; https://physionet.org/content/mimiciv/view-license/0.3/ — *index*.

**eICU Collaborative Research Database — Philips / MIT-LCP.**
Content: Multi-centre US ICU data (208 hospitals) — the geographic-diversity complement to single-centre MIMIC. Access: PhysioNet-credentialed, same pattern as MIMIC; small demo subsets for MIMIC-III and eICU are available *without* credentialing.
Openness: **Same as MIMIC — credentialed, no redistribution; demo subsets BUILD-able for tutorials if their licence is honoured.** Source (comparative access summary, *ricu* docs): https://cran.r-universe.dev/ricu/doc/manual.html — *index*.

**AmsterdamUMCdb — Amsterdam UMC / Amsterdam Medical Data Science.**
Content: First freely accessible EU ICU database; tens of thousands of admissions with demographics, vitals, labs, meds. Access: *not* PhysioNet-hosted — request via Amsterdam Medical Data Science, complete training (e.g. DSOR), receive a download token. Free but individually granted.
Openness: **Credentialed-equivalent — validation-only, no redistribution.** Same source as above — *index*.

**HiRID (High Time Resolution ICU Dataset) — Bern University Hospital.**
Content: >33,000 admissions, high-temporal-resolution ICU time series — the best credentialed set for testing sampling/aliasing behaviour of a physiology engine. Access: PhysioNet-credentialed *plus* an additional access request to the data owners.
Openness: **Credentialed, validation-only, no redistribution.** Same source as above — *index*.

**TCIA — The Cancer Imaging Archive (NCI).**
Content: Public DICOM imaging collections (CT/MR/PET + segmentations, pathology slides) with per-collection DOI citations that *must* be cited on use. Most collections: **CC BY 3.0 / CC BY 4.0**, free to browse/download/use for commercial, scientific and educational purposes, most with **no registration**. Exceptions: a small number of older CC BY-NC collections (NC = no commercial use; no longer offered for new submissions); Limited/Restricted collections (incl. brain/head-neck since 6 Jan 2022, because faces can be reconstructed) under TCIA Limited/Restricted or NCTN licences requiring an account + approved agreement; since April 2025, DUA-requiring datasets are hosted via NCI Cancer Research Data Commons / dbGaP. Universal duties: never attempt to identify/contact participants; never generate facial reconstructions that could identify.
Openness: **BUILD for CC BY collections (attribute + cite dataset DOI + TCIA citation) — the imaging suite's shippable teaching library; EXCLUDE or gate CC BY-NC and Limited collections.** URL: https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/data-usage-policies-and-restrictions/ — *index*.

*What the engine may legitimately validate/train against:* train/ship on VitalDB + PhysioNet Open + TCIA CC BY; validate (off-line, credentialed developer, publish *metrics and code only*) against MIMIC-IV, eICU, HiRID, AmsterdamUMCdb. Any “trained on MIMIC” model weights are a grey zone — the DUA bars sharing the *data*; publish methods/metrics, seek MIT-LCP guidance before distributing weights derived from credentialed data.

### 2. Anesthesia & Critical Care, Deep

**2022 ASA Practice Guidelines for Management of the Difficult Airway — American Society of Anesthesiologists (*Anesthesiology*, Jan 2022; 51 pp.; revision of 2013).**
Content/numbers: New pre-induction *awake-vs-asleep decision tool* — consider awake intubation if difficult laryngoscopy/intubation anticipated **plus any one of**: (1) difficult ventilation (mask/SGA), (2) ↑ aspiration risk, (3) intolerance of brief apnoea / rapid desaturation risk, (4) difficult emergency invasive access. *OPTIMIZE OXYGENATION THROUGHOUT* (low/high-flow nasal O₂ from before airway management through extubation; head-elevated position). Confirm ventilation with **exhaled CO₂ (capnography)** — pulse oximetry lags. *LIMIT ATTEMPTS*: reasonable (not mandatory) limit **3 attempts + 1 by a more skilled clinician** per modality (mask, SGA, intubation); be aware of passage of time; call for help / invasive access in the emergency pathway; consider awakening the patient in the non-emergency cannot-intubate pathway. First-ever **paediatric** difficult-airway infographic + extubation strategy section; video laryngoscopy to be readily available.
Openness: **CITE-ONLY.** ASA guideline + infographics are ASA-copyright (journal paywall ecosystem). Encode the decision logic in original words/diagrams; never ship the ASA infographic images. Summary source (APSF, free to read): https://www.apsf.org/article/anesthesia-patient-safety-foundation-update-2022-american-society-of-anesthesiologists-practice-guidelines-for-management-of-the-difficult-airway/ — *index*.

**ARDSNet ARMA / NIH Ventilator Protocol — NHLBI ARDS Network (*NEJM* 2000; n=861).**
Numbers: Tidal volume dosed to **predicted body weight** (PBW: male 50 + 2.3×[height in − 60]; female 45.5 + 2.3×[height in − 60]). Start **VT 6 mL/kg PBW (protocol range 4–8)** vs traditional 12 mL/kg. **Plateau pressure ≤30 cmH₂O** — if Pplat >30, ↓VT by 1 mL/kg steps to floor 4; if Pplat <25 and VT <6, ↑ by 1 mL/kg back toward 6; VT may rise toward 8 only for severe dyspnoea/breath-stacking or pH <7.15 with Pplat ≤30. Oxygenation goal **PaO₂ 55–80 mmHg / SpO₂ 88–95%** via FiO₂/PEEP table (PEEP 5–24); pH goal 7.30–7.45, RR to max 35. Result: mortality **31.0% vs 39.8%**, more ventilator-free days; stopped early.
Openness: **BUILD the numbers** (US-government network protocol; facts) with citation; do not copy protocol-card artwork verbatim. URL: https://litfl.com/ardsnet-ventilation-strategy/ (LITFL summary of the primary protocol) — *index*.

**Transfusion thresholds — TRICC (Hébert et al., *NEJM* 1999; n=838) → 2023 AABB International Guidelines (*JAMA* 2023; 45 RCTs, 20,599 adults).**
Numbers: TRICC restrictive (**transfuse <7 g/dL, target 7–9**) vs liberal (<10): 30-day mortality 18.7% vs 23.3% (NS overall; in-hospital mortality lower restrictive). AABB 2023 (strong, moderate-certainty): **<7 g/dL** for haemodynamically stable hospitalised adults; **7.5 g/dL** cardiac surgery; **8 g/dL** orthopaedic surgery or pre-existing cardiovascular disease; **<7 g/dL** also for stable critically ill children (with congenital-heart strata 7 / 9 / 7–9 g/dL by repair stage). Caution flag for the case library: MINT (2023, MI + anaemia, n≈3,500) trended *against* restrictive (death/recurrent MI ~17% vs ~15%) — teach acute-MI anaemia as contested, not settled at 7.
Openness: **CITE-ONLY for AABB text** (JAMA/OUP-style paywall; AABB Standards are paid products). Threshold *numbers* are encodable facts. URL (guideline abstract text): https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374681890_Red_Blood_Cell_Transfusion_2023_AABB_International_Guidelines — *index*.

**ICU Liberation Bundle (A–F) — SCCM; grounded in PADIS Guidelines (2018; focused update 2025).**
Content: **A** Assess/prevent/manage pain; **B** Both spontaneous awakening trials + spontaneous breathing trials; **C** Choice of analgesia/sedation (analgesia-first, light sedation, avoid benzodiazepines where possible); **D** Delirium assess/prevent/manage (CAM-ICU/ICDSC); **E** Early mobility/exercise; **F** Family engagement/empowerment. SCCM-cited collaborative evidence (>20,000 patients in secondary summaries): dose-response — higher bundle compliance → lower death, delirium/coma days, restraint use, ICU readmission (teach as association from implementation collaboratives, not an RCT effect size).
Openness: **BUILD the bundle structure as original teaching logic; CITE-ONLY for PADIS/SCCM text.** SCCM bundle materials are freely readable on sccm.org but remain SCCM-copyright. URL: https://sccm.org/blog/exploring-sccm-the-icu-centers-of-excellence-program — *index*.

**ASRA Antithrombotic Guidelines, 5th edition — American Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine (*Reg Anesth Pain Med*, 2025) + ASRA LAST Checklist (2020).**
Numbers (needle placement / neuraxial or *deep plexus/peripheral* blocks — same intervals): rename “prophylactic/therapeutic” → **low dose / high dose**. Enoxaparin low dose **≥12 h**, high dose **≥24 h**; SC heparin 5,000 U 2–3×/day **4–6 h** (or normal coags); apixaban high dose **≥72 h**, low dose (2.5 mg BID) **≥36 h**; rivaroxaban high dose **≥72 h**, low dose (10 mg) **≥24 h** (30 h if CrCl <30); dabigatran high dose **≥72 h** (CrCl ≥50) / **≥120 h** (CrCl 30–49); clopidogrel **5–7 days**; warfarin **5 days + normalised INR**. Acceptable residual: DOAC level **<30 ng/mL** or anti-Xa **≤0.1 IU/mL**. Superficial/compressible peripheral blocks are *not* held to neuraxial intervals (ASRA scope limit; Tsui/CAS advisory stratifies the rest). LAST: stop injection, call for help, **20% lipid emulsion early**, modified ACLS (epinephrine ≤1 mcg/kg, avoid vasopressin/CCB/beta-blocker/lidocaine).
Openness: **CITE-ONLY** (ASRA/RAPM copyright; ASRA checklists/apps are distributed products). Encode intervals as a queryable table in original form with “verify against current ASRA guideline” labelling. URLs: https://lms.ehc.gov.eg/lms/mod/book/tool/print/index.php?id=742 (executive summary mirror); https://www.forgeanesthesia.com/nce-regional-anesthesia.html (LAST + intervals summary) — *index*.

**RASS — Richmond Agitation–Sedation Scale (Sessler et al., 2002, Virginia Commonwealth University).**
Numbers: 10 points, **−5 to +4**: +4 combative, +3 very agitated, +2 agitated, +1 restless, **0 alert & calm**, −1 drowsy (eye contact >10 s to voice), −2 light sedation (<10 s), −3 moderate (movement/eye opening, no eye contact), −4 deep (responds only to physical stimulus), −5 unarousable. Usual ICU target in PADIS-era practice: light sedation **−2 to 0/+1** unless deep sedation specifically indicated. Assessment order: observe → voice → physical stimulus.
Openness: **BUILD — the scale is freely reproduced in practice** (Physiopedia lists it as freely available online); still credit Sessler/VCU and reproduce wording minimally/paraphrased where possible. URL: https://www.physio-pedia.com/Richmond_Agitation-Sedation_Scale_(RASS) — *index*.

**Vasopressors — SOAP II (De Backer et al., *NEJM* 2010; n=1,679) + Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 (SCCM/ESICM).**
Numbers: SOAP II dopamine vs norepinephrine: 28-day death 52.5% vs 48.5% (OR 1.17, p=0.10, NS) but arrhythmic events **24.1% vs 12.4% (p<0.001)**; cardiogenic-shock subgroup mortality higher with dopamine. SSC 2021 order: **(1) norepinephrine** first-line, **MAP target ≥65 mmHg**; **(2) add vasopressin 0.03 units/min** (fixed; rather than escalating NE beyond ~0.25–0.5 mcg/kg/min); **(3) add epinephrine**; dobutamine added for cardiac dysfunction/hypoperfusion despite MAP; hydrocortisone 200 mg/day in ongoing refractory shock. Hour-1 bundle context: lactate, cultures before antibiotics, broad-spectrum antibiotics, **30 mL/kg crystalloid** for hypotension/lactate ≥4, vasopressors for MAP <65 (teach 30 mL/kg as the 2021 “suggested” starting volume — contested since, see CLOVERS/CLASSIC-era debate; do not hard-code as unquestionable).
Openness: **CITE-ONLY** (NEJM / *Crit Care Med* texts). Numbers encodable. URLs: https://www.uspharmacist.com/article/vasopressor-use-in-the-icu ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/articles/PMC13464546/ — *index*.

### 3. Emergency & Resuscitation

**AHA ACLS / BLS — 2020 AHA Guidelines for CPR & ECC (*Circulation* 2020; still the cited base with focused updates).**
Numbers (adult): compressions **100–120/min**, depth **≥5 cm, ≤6 cm (2–2.4 in)**, full recoil, interruptions **<10 s**, rotate compressors q2 min; ratio 30:2 without advanced airway, continuous + 1 breath/6 s with. Defibrillation VF/pVT: **biphasic 120–200 J** (manufacturer dose; escalate same/higher), **monophasic 360 J**. Epinephrine **1 mg IV/IO q3–5 min** — *as early as feasible in non-shockable* rhythms; in shockable rhythms after initial defibrillation attempts fail. Amiodarone **300 mg** (2nd dose 150 mg) or lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg for shock-refractory VF/pVT. Synchronised cardioversion reference points: AF 120–200 J biphasic, SVT/flutter 50–100 J.
**PALS (2020):** rate 100–120/min; depth **≥⅓ AP diameter** (≈5 cm child, ≈4 cm infant); 2-rescuer ratio **15:2**; defibrillation **2 J/kg → 4 J/kg → ≥4 up to max 10 J/kg** (or adult dose); epinephrine **0.01 mg/kg IV/IO q3–5 min** (max 1 mg); amiodarone **5 mg/kg**; synchronised cardioversion **0.5–1 J/kg** (then 2 J/kg).
Openness: **CITE-ONLY, strictly.** AHA guideline *articles* are readable in *Circulation*; AHA course books, algorithm posters/cards and eLearning are **commercial products** — never ship scans/redrawn copies of AHA algorithms. Re-draw original flowcharts encoding the same numbers, labelled “consistent with AHA 2020” with citation. URLs (secondary summaries agreeing on all numbers above): https://deepmedicine.in/clinical/protocols/acls-algorithm.html ; https://www.statpearls.com/ArticleLibrary/viewarticle/18926 — *index*.

**Stroke — NIHSS + AHA/ASA Early Management 2019 (Powers et al.).**
Numbers: **NIHSS: 15 items, 0–42** (0 none; 1–4 minor; 5–15 moderate; 16–20 moderate-severe; 21–42 severe; ≥4-point change = clinically significant). IV thrombolysis (alteplase; tenecteplase increasingly used): **within 4.5 h** of last-known-well in eligible patients (BP <185/110, glucose, anticoagulant, and imaging exclusions apply — encode the full checklist, not the window alone). Thrombectomy: **NIHSS ≥6 + ASPECTS ≥6 + ICA/M1 occlusion within 6 h** standard; **up to 24 h** in DAWN/DEFUSE-3-selected patients (clinical/imaging mismatch, small core/large penumbra); basilar: NIHSS ≥10 within 24 h in trial-selected patients. System targets to teach alongside: door-to-needle ≤60 min, door-to-imaging ≤25 min (standard stroke-centre metrics).
Openness: **NIHSS itself: BUILD** (US-government scale, in the public domain in practice; training/certification programmes around it are commercial — ship the scale, not a certification course). **AHA/ASA guideline text: CITE-ONLY.** URL: https://www.sismed-it.com/en/nihss-stroke-scale-calculator/ — *index*.

**STEMI — ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI ACS Guideline 2025 (replaces 2013 STEMI guideline).**
Numbers: ECG **≤10 min** from first medical contact (FMC). Primary PCI: **FMC/door-to-device ≤90 min** (direct presenters); transfer: **FMC-to-device ≤120 min** and **door-in–door-out ≤30 min** at the non-PCI hospital; if PCI cannot meet 120 min → **fibrinolysis door-to-needle ≤30 min**, then transfer (pharmaco-invasive/rescue PCI 3–24 h if failed). Radial access preferred; complete revascularisation of non-culprit lesions in stable multivessel STEMI upgraded to Class I in 2025.
Openness: **CITE-ONLY** (ACC/AHA *JACC/Circulation*). Numbers encodable. URL: https://sattimd.com/guidelines/acc-acs-2025.html — *index*.

**Tranexamic acid — CRASH-2 (*Lancet* 2010) & WOMAN (*Lancet* 2017), LSHTM-led.**
Numbers: CRASH-2: n=**20,211**, 274 hospitals/40 countries, TXA **1 g IV over 10 min + 1 g over 8 h**, randomised within 8 h. All-cause mortality **14.5% vs 16.0% (RR 0.91; NNT ≈67)**; death from bleeding 4.9% vs 5.7% (RR 0.85). Time interaction is the teaching point: benefit concentrated **≤3 h** (≈⅓ reduction in bleeding death ≤1 h); **>3 h: no benefit / possible harm — do not give late.** WOMAN: n=**20,060**, 193 hospitals/21 countries, TXA **1 g IV** (± second 1 g) for postpartum haemorrhage: death from bleeding **1.5% vs 1.9% (RR 0.81)**; **within 3 h of birth RR 0.69**; hysterectomy *not* reduced (RR 1.02).
Openness: **CITE-ONLY for Lancet texts; numbers BUILD.** (CRASH trials' educational summaries from LSHTM are freely shared; still cite, don't copy.) URLs: https://coreem.net/journal-reviews/crash-2-tranexamic-acid-in-major-trauma/ ; https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(17)30638-4/fulltext — *index*.

**TCCC vs civilian care — Committee on TCCC / Joint Trauma System (guidelines continuously updated; current version via Deployed Medicine/JTS).**
Differences to encode as *contrasting rule-sets*, not one “trauma mode”: phases **Care Under Fire → Tactical Field Care → Tactical Evacuation Care** (no civilian analogue); **MARCH** priority with immediate high-and-tight limb tourniquets + haemostatic dressings *before* airway (civilian ATLS historically airway-first, though civilian practice has converged toward catastrophic-haemorrhage-first/C-ABC — teach the convergence honestly); permissive hypotension / whole-blood-forward resuscitation, TXA + calcium early, ketamine-forward analgesia, early broad-spectrum antibiotics for penetrating wounds, minimal crystalloid, aggressive hypothermia prevention, prolonged-field-care expectations (hours–days) vs civilian short transport + ATLS/definitive-care timelines; tourniquet *conversion/reposition* ideally **<2 h**, do not convert after **>6 h** in the field per current TCCC teaching. Note: a May 2026 TCCC revision was reported in secondary sources this round (expanded conversion to lower tiers, analgesia/antibiotic changes) — **flagged as index-only, single-source; verify against the current JTS guideline PDF before encoding 2026 specifics.**
Openness: **BUILD-leaning** — TCCC guidelines are US-military public works, distributed free (Deployed Medicine app/site); cite JTS/CoTCCC and date-stamp the version encoded, because TCCC changes faster than any civilian guideline. URL: https://tccc.org.ua/en/guide/introduction-to-tccc (mirror of TCCC intro curriculum) — *index*.

**Toxicology antidotes — TOXBASE / UK National Poisons Information Service (NPIS).**
Content: ~21,000 product entries; the UK first-line clinical toxicology database (antidotes, antivenoms, pregnancy, chemicals). Access: **free only to NHS/UKHSA/MOD/ac.uk registrants**; departments/overseas by **annual subscription**; app free for eligible users, £6.99/yr limited version for others; **not available for public access**. US analogue (PoisonHelp / regional poison centres) is phone-service, not an open database; EXTOXNET/HSDB remnants and LITFL/EMCrit toxicology libraries are the open teaching layer.
Openness: **CLOSED for TOXBASE content — cite-only, never scrape or embed monographs.** Build the antidote table (agent → antidote → dose) from primary drug labelling/open references (e.g. NAC 150+50+100 mg/kg 21-h regimen, naloxone, sodium bicarbonate for TCA with QRS >100 ms — as surfaced in open EM summaries) with “check TOXBASE/poison centre” behaviour taught in-case. URLs: https://www.toxbase.org/information-for-toxbase-subscribers/ ; https://www.npis.org/Toxbase.html — *index*.

### 4. Surgical Subspecialty Evidence

**Vascular — NASCET/ECST carotid + AAA thresholds.**
Numbers: NASCET % stenosis = (1 − narrowest lumen ÷ normal *distal* ICA) ×100 (ECST uses estimated bulb diameter; ECST ≈ 0.6×NASCET + 40, so 70% NASCET ≈ 82% ECST — *always label the method*). Symptomatic: **70–99% NASCET → CEA, ideally within 2 weeks** (2-yr ipsilateral stroke 9% vs 26%; NNT ≈6); **50–69% modest benefit** (select: men, recent event, high-risk features); **<50% or occlusion → no surgery**. Doppler SRU consensus for ≥70%: ICA PSV **>230 cm/s**, ICA/CCA **>4.0**, EDV **>100 cm/s**. Asymptomatic: modern best-medical-therapy era — present CEA/CAS for ≥60–70% as *selected/contested*, not automatic. AAA (UK Small Aneurysm + ADAM): surveillance safe to **5.5 cm**; elective repair threshold **≥5.5 cm men, ≥5.0 cm women** in contemporary guidance, or growth >1 cm/yr, or any symptomatic/ruptured AAA → emergency repair.
Openness: **BUILD numbers** (trial facts; NICE NG128 freely readable). URLs: https://thoracickey.com/vascular-trials/ ; http://radiogyan.com/nascet-calculator/ — *index*.

**Cardiothoracic basics — CABG conduit evidence (overview level only).**
Numbers: **LIMA→LAD is the gold-standard conduit**: 10-yr patency ~**87–96%** (commonly quoted ~95%) vs saphenous vein **55–74%** at 10 yrs; radial artery intermediate (~88% in comparative cohorts). SYNTAX/FREEDOM lineage: CABG preferred over PCI for **left-main / three-vessel disease with intermediate–high SYNTAX (>22, esp. >32)** and for **diabetic multivessel disease** (FREEDOM) — teach Heart-Team/SYNTAX framing, not a single cut-off rule.
Openness: **CITE-ONLY** (journal literature). URL: https://www.mayoclinicproceedings.org/article/S0025-6196(22)00586-9/fulltext?sf174683232=1 — *index*.

**Neurosurgery — GCS + Brain Trauma Foundation Severe TBI, 4th ed. (2017).**
Numbers: **GCS = E(1–4) + V(1–5) + M(1–6) = 3–15** (Teasdale & Jennett, 1974); bands **13–15 mild, 9–12 moderate, 3–8 severe**; report components (e.g. E2V4M3), never the total alone; GCS ≤8 = “intubate” heuristic (teach as heuristic, not law — decision is airway/ventilation-based). BTF: **treat ICP >22 mmHg**; **CPP target 60–70 mmHg** (CPP = MAP − ICP); SBP **≥100 mmHg (age 50–69), ≥110 mmHg (age 15–49 or >70)**; ICP monitoring in salvageable GCS 3–8 with abnormal CT, or normal CT + ≥2 of (age >40, SBP <90, posturing); avoid prolonged prophylactic hyperventilation; 7 days seizure prophylaxis (phenytoin/levetiracetam era) for early post-traumatic seizures.
Openness: **GCS: BUILD** (freely reproduced scale). **BTF text: CITE-ONLY** (Foundation/*Neurosurgery*); numbers encodable. URLs: https://www.solvetutor.com/gcs/ ; https://sicu.eastbaysurgery.com/content/severe-tbi — *index*.

**Orthopedics — Garden, Pauwels, compartment syndrome.**
Numbers: **Garden I–IV** (intracapsular femoral neck): I incomplete/valgus-impacted, II complete undisplaced, III complete partially displaced, IV complete fully displaced (I–II “undisplaced” → fixation in most; III–IV → arthroplasty in older patients, urgent fixation in the young). **Pauwels** (fracture-line angle to horizontal): **I <30°, II 30–50°, III >50°** — rising shear/instability/non-union risk with angle. **Compartment syndrome:** normal compartment pressure <10 mmHg; fasciotomy threshold **ΔP = diastolic BP − compartment pressure ≤30 mmHg** (McQueen) — *ΔP, not absolute pressure, is the reliable trigger*; absolute >30 mmHg alone over-diagnoses. Clinical 5–6 Ps are late/unreliable in the obtunded — measure.

### 5. Tissue Biomechanics & Surgical Physics

> **Honesty note (must ship with the engine):** there is no single “modulus of liver.” Published values for the same tissue routinely span **an order of magnitude** with method (indentation vs rheometry vs elastography), frequency/strain, perfusion, temperature, species, and disease. Shear modulus G and Young's modulus E differ by ~3× for incompressible tissue (E ≈ 3G) and are routinely conflated in secondary tables. The engine should store **ranges + method tags**, expose the range in teacher view, and tune *feel/relative ordering* (brain ≪ liver < muscle ≪ cartilage ≪ bone), not pretend to Pa-level truth.

**Elastic moduli — indicative Young's modulus E ranges (compiled mechanobiology literature).**
Brain **0.1–1 kPa** (some rheometry 1–3 kPa); liver **0.3–6 kPa** (healthy elastography ~<5–6.5 kPa; fibrotic 3–22 kPa shear-derived; cirrhosis >12 kPa, severe disease >40 kPa reported); lung 0.2–2 kPa (fibrotic ~17 kPa); kidney 4–8 kPa; vessel wall **1–10 kPa at low strain** (layered/anisotropic; MPa-range at high/physiological tensile strain — the widest method trap in the table); skeletal muscle **8–17 kPa** passive; skin **MPa range** (abdominal epidermis ~6 MPa young vs >10 MPa older/drier — skin must not be modelled with kPa soft-tissue defaults); cartilage bulk **25 kPa–>1 MPa** (pericellular matrix far softer); ligament **25–93 MPa**; osteoid 25–40 kPa; **calcified bone 1–20 GPa** (~6 orders of magnitude above brain).
Openness: **BUILD** (open-access reviews, CC BY). URLs: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-022-00412-z?error=cookies_not_supported&code=0dd99fa4-e844-481c-90ed-9ea274ba7b63 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/articles/PMC3641165/ ; https://www.cellgs.com/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-elasticity-for-cell-culture.html — *index*.

**Suture properties — retention, not just breaking strength, is the simulatable variable.**
Numbers (tensile-strength retention / absorption): Vicryl (polyglactin 910, braided): **75% @2 wk, 50% @3 wk, 25% @4 wk**; absorbed 56–70 d. Monocryl (poliglecaprone, mono): ~**50–60% @1 wk, 20–30% @2 wk**, gone by ~3 wk; absorbed 91–119 d. PDS II (polydioxanone, mono): **70% @2 wk, 50% @4 wk, 25% @6 wk**; absorbed 180–210 d — the prolonged-support choice (fascia). Plain gut 7–10 d strength; chromic gut 21–28 d. Silk: non-absorbable but loses ~50% by 1 yr, high tissue reactivity. Nylon: ~20% loss/yr. Polypropylene/steel: indefinite. Handling rules to encode: monofilament = low drag/low infection harbouring, more throws needed, memory; braided = better knot security/handling, more drag/reactivity; standard teaching = 4 throws for most synthetics in the cited university tables (knot security is material-specific — do not universalise).
Openness: **BUILD the property table as facts** (manufacturer datasheets + university teaching tables); brand names (Vicryl/Monocryl/PDS are Ethicon trademarks) — use generic names first, brands parenthetically. URLs: https://www.gynecoloncol.com/surgery/suture-materials-suture-size-needles-clinical-significance/ ; https://wcvm.usask.ca/vsac205/Lab4/suture-materials.php — *index*.

**Electrosurgery physics — SAGES Fundamentals of Laparoscopic Surgery (FLS) canon.**
Numbers/logic: ESU converts mains to high-frequency RF. **Cut:** continuous, *unmodulated, low-voltage* waveform → rapid heating, intracellular water → steam, cell vaporisation; minimal lateral damage, poor haemostasis; electrode hovers (no direct contact). **Coag:** *intermittent, damped, high-peak-voltage* waveform (low duty cycle) → slower heating, desiccation/fulguration, shallow eschar, better haemostasis, more spread. **Blend:** alternating cut/coag duty cycles. Protein denaturation/coagulation begins **>60 °C**; vaporisation at **100 °C**. Current density ∝ power, ∝ 1/area² — small tip = cutting density, large dispersive pad = safe return (pad off hair/bony prominence/scar, over vascular muscle). Laparoscopic hazards to simulate: insulation failure, capacitive coupling, direct coupling. Bipolar: current only between jaws, lower energy, seals vessels **up to 7 mm** (advanced bipolar, impedance-feedback). Ultrasonic: **~55 kHz** (FLS teaching: 50,000 Hz class) mechanical vibration, no patient current, blade temps to ~105 °C.
**Thermal spread (measured, porcine/human literature):** histologic depth ~**0.4–1.2 mm** in head-to-head porcine work at 30 W (monopolar ovary 0.99±0.82 mm; bipolar uterus 1.15±0.2 mm; ultrasonic uterus 0.48±0.23 mm; plasma lowest) — while *lateral* spread across human-tissue studies spans **0–7.64 mm** by device/tissue/method (2026 systematic review: no reliable device ranking possible). Monopolar tissue >42 °C detectable **1 cm** from the tip in cited porcine work. Engine rule: model spread in **single-digit mm**, monopolar > bipolar ≈ ultrasonic on average, with wide variance bands — any product claiming “device X spreads exactly Y mm” is overstating the literature.
Openness: **FLS curriculum itself: CLOSED** (SAGES/ACS paid programme — teach the physics, don't copy FLS modules/tests). Physics facts + journal numbers: **BUILD**. URLs: https://www.imrpress.com/journal/CEOG/46/6/10.12891/ceog5061.2019/htm ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/42869973/ ; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-19-3755-2_4 — *index*.

**Laparoscopic insufflation — pressures & physiology.**
Numbers: CO₂ (non-flammable, soluble — embolism-tolerant relative to air), working pressure **12–15 mmHg** adults (FLS max preset **15 mmHg**; total volume typically 1–3 L at establishment in teaching texts); low-pressure surgery 6–8 mmHg studied; paediatric lower (infants ~8 mmHg; 10–12 mmHg older children in cited teaching). Physiology the engine must couple: at ~12 mmHg ↑CVP, ↑SVR (to ~+65%), ↑PVR (to ~+90% in cited anaesthesia reviews); **≥15 mmHg IVC compression → ↓venous return → ↓cardiac output (to −50% in reverse-Trendelenburg/low-reserve patients)**; diaphragm splinting → ↓FRC/compliance, ↑peak airway pressure, V/Q mismatch; peritoneal CO₂ absorption → hypercapnia/respiratory acidosis unless minute ventilation ↑; renal blood flow ↓35–60%, oliguria; vagal bradycardia on rapid insufflation. Normal resting IAP <5 mmHg for contrast.
Openness: **BUILD** (open anaesthesia literature). URL: https://www.Intechopen.com/chapters/18348 — *index*.

### 6. Laboratory Medicine & Pathophysiology Depth

**Cell-based model of coagulation — Hoffman & Monroe, *Thromb Haemost* 2001.**
Content: Replaces the 1964 Davie–Ratnoff/Macfarlane cascade *as the in-vivo model* (cascade retained as the correct model of PT/aPTT assays). Three overlapping phases on cell surfaces: **(1) Initiation** on the tissue-factor-bearing cell (TF–VIIa → IXa/Xa → picomolar thrombin); **(2) Amplification** (thrombin activates platelets + V/VIII/XI, VIII released from vWF); **(3) Propagation** on the activated platelet surface (IXa–VIIIa tenase + Xa–Va prothrombinase → thrombin burst → fibrin). This is the model that explains why haemophilia bleeds despite an intact “extrinsic” pathway, and why PT/aPTT can mislead about in-vivo bleeding — the causal-chain structure the reasoning engine should implement, with cascade view kept as the “lab-test view.”
Openness: **BUILD the model logic** (facts/ideas); the 2001 paper text/figures are publisher-copyright — redraw original diagrams. URL: https://derangedphysiology.com/main/cicm-primary-exam/haematological-system/Chapter-018/haemostasis-and-coagulation (open teaching synthesis citing Hoffman) — *index*.

**Reference-range methodology — CLSI EP28-A3c, *Defining, Establishing, and Verifying Reference Intervals* (2010, reaffirmed 2020).**
Numbers: Reference interval = **central 95%** of a carefully defined healthy reference population, limits at the **2.5th and 97.5th percentiles** — by definition **5% of healthy people fall outside** (2.5% each tail); “abnormal flag ≠ disease” must be a first-class engine concept. Parametric alternative (Gaussian): mean ±1.96 SD, ≥40 observations in teaching summaries.

### 9. Global & Resource-Tiered Medicine

**Lancet Commission on Global Surgery — *Global Surgery 2030* (*Lancet* 2015, Meara et al.).**

**ASCO Resource-Stratified Guidelines — American Society of Clinical Oncology (framework since 2016).**

*What low-resource design changes in the teaching system (synthesis from the three sources above):* (1) every procedure wave needs an **equipment-tier variant** (no piped O₂ / no ventilator / no blood bank / no CT branches — e.g. torsion and AAA decisions without Doppler/CT, ketamine-based anaesthesia, non-operative options where evidence supports them); (2) teach **Bellwether-first** prioritisation and task-sharing/generalist-team realities; (3) time/distance and cost-to-patient must be modelled variables (a “correct” referral the patient cannot afford/reach is a failed plan — catastrophic-expenditure framing); (4) improvisation must be taught as *standards with evidence* (e.g. WHO Surgical Safety Checklist — free WHO material — works without any capital equipment), never as “anything goes.”

### 10. Regulatory & Ethics Boundaries for Educational Medical Software

*Careful statement, sourced — this section is orientation, not legal advice; classification turns on the manufacturer's stated intended use.*

**FDA Clinical Decision Support Guidance — Final Guidance, 28 Sep 2022 (interpreting FD&C Act §520(o)(1)(E), added by the 21st Century Cures Act).**
Rule: a CDS software function is **Non-Device CDS** (outside device regulation) **only if ALL FOUR criteria hold**: **(1)** it does *not* acquire/process/analyse a medical image, an IVD signal, or a pattern/signal from a signal-acquisition system; **(2)** it displays/analyses/prints *medical information* about a patient or other medical information (studies, guidelines); **(3)** it *supports/provides recommendations to a healthcare professional* about prevention/diagnosis/treatment; **(4)** it enables that professional to *independently review the basis* for the recommendations, so they do not rely primarily on the software for the decision. Fail any one → device CDS (FDA-regulated). FDA's 2022 reading is *narrow*: patient-specific directive outputs, time-critical alarming, and image/signal processing push functions into device territory; software aimed at *patients/consumers* (not HCPs) cannot use this carve-out at all.
*Where Anatomica sits:* a simulator operating on **fictional/synthetic patients for education** is not CDS about a real patient at all — the cleanest position, and the one to state in the intended-use text. Two tripwires: (a) any feature that ingests a **real** patient's image/waveform for interpretation fails Criterion 1 by design; (b) marketing language (“use at the bedside to decide…”) can re-characterise an educational tool as intended for real-patient decisions. Keep “for education/simulation only; not for use in patient care” as a functional boundary (no real-patient data entry pathways), not just a footer. URL: https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/fda_issues_final_guidance_clinical_decision_support_software_1022.html ; corroborating summary: https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/10_17-fda-issues-final-clinical-decision — *index*.

**EU MDR — Regulation (EU) 2017/745, Annex VIII Rule 11 (applies since 26 May 2021).**
Rule (overview): software is a *device* only if the manufacturer intends a medical purpose in Art. 2(1) (diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment…). If it is device software: information used for diagnostic/therapeutic decisions = **Class IIa** minimum; → **Class IIb** if decisions could cause *serious deterioration or a surgical intervention*; → **Class III** if *death or irreversible deterioration*; monitoring physiological processes = **IIa**, or **IIb** for vital parameters whose variation could mean *immediate danger*; all other device software = Class I. IIa+ requires Notified Body conformity assessment (this “up-classification” vs the old Directive is the headline change; MDCG 2019-11 is the operative qualification/classification guidance).
*Where Anatomica sits:* software *intended purely for education/training* with no real-patient purpose is generally **outside MDR device scope** (qualification follows intended purpose) — the same intended-use discipline as in the US, with the same tripwires, and a *lower* tolerance once any real-patient decision/monitoring purpose is claimed (IIa floor, realistically IIb for physiology-monitoring claims). Do not market an EU “training” build with clinical-performance claims. URL: https://www.meddevicecorp.com/mdr-classification/ — *index*.

**Informed-consent teaching standards (for the cases module).** No single open “consent standard” document governs internationally; the teachable, sourceable core is: consent = **capacity + disclosure of material risks/benefits/alternatives (incl. no treatment) + voluntariness + authorisation**, with jurisdiction-specific materiality tests (US *Canterbury* reasonable-patient lineage; UK *Montgomery v Lanarkshire* [2015] — what a reasonable person *in this patient's position* would attach significance to). For simulation specifically: learners must consent to recording/assessment use of their performance data, simulated patients/actors require explicit consent and role-protection, and *no real patient* may be recorded or used as teaching data without consent + governance approval — mirror these rules inside the product's own data practices (synthetic cases only by default).

**Ethics of simulation replacing early patient contact — literature position.**
Consensus shape (Gaba definition lineage; SBME ethics reviews): simulation is “a technique… to **replace or amplify** real experiences with guided experiences” — the ethical justification is **non-maleficence**: shifting *first attempts* off patients (deliberate practice to a defined standard before supervised patient contact) reduces trainee-inflicted harm and standardises exposure (justice: every learner meets the rare emergency, not only the lucky rotation). Meta-analytic teaching summaries cite deliberate-practice simulation improving performance ~20–30% and reducing procedural errors (up to ~38% in cited syntheses — present as review-level claims). The boundary the literature defends with equal force: simulation **prepares for, and must not wholly replace**, supervised real-patient experience — transfer, professional socialisation, and capability under genuine uncertainty require clinical placement; programmes that substitute simulation hours wholesale for clinical exposure without transfer evidence overclaim. Product rule that follows: label every competency “practised in simulation” vs “requires supervised clinical sign-off,” and never issue language implying clinical competence/certification from simulator completion alone.
