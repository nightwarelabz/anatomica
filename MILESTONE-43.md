# Milestone 43 — Case #5: ulnar neuropathy localization

**Status: built and verified locally; not uploaded.** The Case
Workbench gains its fifth case and its second localization case:
`case.ulnar-neuropathy.005` — ulnar-distribution hand symptoms in a
58-year-old man, where the diagnostic question is not *which nerve*
but *which level*: cubital tunnel (elbow) against Guyon's canal
(wrist), C8 radiculopathy, lower-trunk/medial-cord plexopathy and
neurogenic thoracic outlet syndrome. The discriminating content is
branch anatomy, taught as anatomy: the dorsal ulnar cutaneous
branch, the forearm motor branches, the palmar cutaneous branch,
and the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve that never travels with
the ulnar nerve at all. The case continues Milestone 41's pattern
exactly — board likelihood ratios only from sources consulted
in-session, the criteria machinery reused, an honest imaging
assessment, exactly one anchoring event — with the anchoring
structure deliberately inverted (below), and one instrument new to
the module: the McGowan severity grade, mechanically evaluated by a
declared `classification` block that grades severity and is never
allowed to localize.

## 2. Design rationale

Case 4 asked *which structure, at the one site in question* — median
nerve at the wrist, against a neck the history had framed. Case 5
asks the prior question a localization service actually lives on:
**one nerve, two entrapment sites, three proximal mimics** — and the
answer is carried by which *branches* are involved, because a
branch's involvement places the lesion relative to that branch's
takeoff. The patient was designed to make the wrong answer
attractive: a long-distance truck driver (prolonged elbow flexion,
elbow resting on the cab door frame — an elbow-level exposure) who
is also a weekend road cyclist on drop handlebars (sustained
weight-bearing through the hypothenar hand — the classic Guyon's
canal exposure, "cyclist's palsy"). Both exposure stories are true,
both are in the stage-1 history, and the case asserts causation for
neither: exposures raise priors, they do not localize lesions. The
localization is made on branch anatomy (stage 2), segmental
enlargement (stage 3) and a practice-parameter electrodiagnostic
protocol (stage 4). Deliberate contrasts with case 4: no diabetes
(the diffuse-neuropathy pull is absent by history and confirmed
absent electrically), no labs at all, no cervical MRI (the menu's
wasted order is a wrist ultrasound instead — §6), and a criteria
block that grades rather than diagnoses (§5).

## 3. The localization logic, stated as anatomy

Each discriminator works because of where a branch leaves the
nerve. The ulnar nerve is the continuation of the medial cord
(C8, T1); behind the elbow it traverses the cubital tunnel, pierces
the two heads of flexor carpi ulnaris, runs the forearm, gives off
— proximal to distal — branches to flexor carpi ulnaris and the
medial half of flexor digitorum profundus, then the palmar
cutaneous branch, then the dorsal cutaneous branch, and only then
crosses the wrist superficial to the flexor retinaculum through
Guyon's canal, where it divides into superficial (palmar medial
1½ fingers) and deep motor (intrinsic hand muscles) branches —
the course and distributions of the linked clinical record
`major.ulnar` (M39, source C30), whose `clinical_testing` field
states the elbow/wrist patterns verbatim.

- **Dorsal ulnar cutaneous branch → elbow-or-above, never isolated
  wrist.** The branch leaves the nerve in the forearm, proximal to
  Guyon's canal, and supplies the dorsal medial hand and dorsal
  medial 1½ fingers. A lesion inside the canal anatomically cannot
  touch it; its involvement (stage-2 examination finding, and its
  SNAP at stage 4) excludes an isolated wrist lesion outright. The
  record states the contrapositive: in a wrist lesion the dorsal
  cutaneous branch is *spared* and sensory loss is confined to the
  palmar medial 1½ fingers. This is the case's decisive finding —
  and it carries **no likelihood ratio**, because no consulted
  source publishes an accuracy pair for it. Its force is not
  statistical; a wrist lesion cannot produce it at all.
- **Flexor carpi ulnaris / medial flexor digitorum profundus →
  proximal to the wrist.** Their branches leave the nerve in the
  forearm. Weakness in either is impossible from a canal lesion,
  whose motor pattern is intrinsic hand muscles only (C30). The
  second anatomical leg against the wrist; also unquantified, for
  the same stated reason.
- **Palmar cutaneous + superficial branch pattern → ulnar nerve,
  either level.** Medial palm and palmar medial 1½ finger loss is
  expected in both patterns; it confirms the nerve, not the level,
  and the case says so. (Contrast case 4, where the palmar
  cutaneous branch's *extra*-tunnel course made thenar-eminence
  sparing a positive localizer for the carpal tunnel.)
- **Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve → against plexus and
  outlet.** The MABC is a medial-cord branch carrying lower-trunk
  fibres that never enter the ulnar nerve. A lower-trunk or
  medial-cord lesion severe enough to denervate ulnar intrinsics
  would be expected to involve it; its territory is clinically
  intact (stage 2) and its SNAP is normal (stage 4). This pair is
  the plexopathy/NTOS exclusion — and the electrodiagnostic order
  is classified safety-net with the plexopathy as its named
  must-not-miss precisely because this leg is the parameter's
  designed exclusion work.
- **Ulnar SNAP reduced → postganglionic; against the root.** A C8
  root lesion sits proximal to the dorsal root ganglion and leaves
  the sensory SNAP intact; a reduced ulnar SNAP places the sensory
  lesion in nerve or plexus. Paired with a needle map confined to
  ulnar muscles (non-ulnar C8 muscles and cervical paraspinals
  normal — the extension the AAEM parameter prescribes, C31), the
  radiculopathy falls by conjunction.
- **Froment's and Wartenberg's signs → ulnar motor involvement,
  non-localizing.** Both read the intrinsic hand muscles, which are
  ulnar at either level. The case states their non-localizing
  nature in their own entries — they grade (Froment's muscle is a
  McGowan input by way of the weakness finding), they do not place.
- **Tinel's sign at the elbow, absent at Guyon's canal; combined
  pressure-flexion at the elbow.** Percussion speaks at the level
  percussed: the percussion map points at one level only. The
  elbow Tinel carries the board's largest LR (§4, with its caveat);
  the combined pressure-flexion manoeuvre — the most sensitive
  provocative test at 30 seconds in the consulted primary study
  (0.91) — corroborates qualitatively and carries no LR, because
  that study's consulted report does not state its specificity.
- **Inching → the segment itself.** The practice parameter's
  listed option (C31): 1 cm stimulation increments place the
  maximal latency/amplitude change at the medial epicondyle
  segment. Its verdict is a place, not a probability; no LR exists
  for it in any consulted source.

## 5. The McGowan block — a grade that is not allowed to localize

Case 4's CTS-6 both diagnosed and graded. This case's instrument
only grades: the McGowan classification (C34) is evaluated by a
declared `classification` block from four inputs — paresthesia,
objective sensory loss, motor weakness, interosseous paralysis —
under three ordered rules. At stage 1 it is **pending** ("not yet
classifiable — the motor examination that separates the grades is
not yet on record"): the grade turns on weakness and paralysis, and
a severity grade is not a history product. From stage 2 it is
**grade II (intermediate)**: objective sensory loss and 4/5 weakness
present, early wasting present, and — the grade-III boundary — an
*examined absence* of paralysis, recorded as a finding
(`f.interosseous-paralysis`, presence `absent`) rather than left as
missing data, the discipline of case 4's thenar finding. The
adjudication carries the comparison under `criteria_comparison`
(deliberately not the key `classification`, which the machinery
reserves for Strasberg-style results) and states the separation the
whole case teaches: **the grade says how much nerve; the board says
which level.** A grade II lesion can sit at either site, and grading
this patient at stage 2 localized nothing.

## 6. The anchoring event — self-installed, and surviving a
refutation the learner elicited himself

Exactly one event is scored, recomputed by the checker:
**(stage 3, hyp.ulnar-neuropathy-wrist), learner 0.16 against a
workbench 0.0024.** The structure is the deliberate inverse of case
4's event in every particular:

- **Installation.** Case 4's anchor was installed by another
  clinician before the case began and fed by artifacts
  (renormalization, a degenerative image). Case 5's is
  **self-installed at stage 1 from a true fact**: the patient is a
  cyclist, cyclists do get Guyon's canal compression, and the
  learner led his board with the wrist (0.34) on the exposure story
  alone — while the same history contained the elbow story
  (flexion-provoked nocturnal symptoms, the door-frame habit) that
  he underweighted (elbow 0.16).
- **The refutation.** At stage 2 the learner's own examination
  elicited the dorsal cutaneous finding — a branch-anatomy
  exclusion of the wrist, not a soft sign — and his own wrist
  ultrasound returned a normal canal. He recorded the dorsal
  finding *as* a soft sign, kept the wrist second (0.28), and the
  anchor survived a refutation he personally produced. No artifact
  fed it: nothing at stage 2 argued *for* the wrist at all.
- **The scoring stage.** The event lands at stage 3, not 4: the
  elbow ultrasound retires the wrist on the workbench board
  (0.0212 → 0.0024) and the learner, conceding the elbow to 0.66,
  still holds 0.16 at the canal — the anchor's half-life, one stage
  after its anatomical death. By stage 4 the inching study and the
  dorsal cutaneous SNAP (the electrical restatement of the stage-2
  finding) close it; he commits correctly and concedes the wrist to
  0.04.

Guardrails held by construction: the residuals the workbench
retires at stage 2 (C8, plexopathy, NTOS) are held by the learner
below the 0.10 anchoring threshold at every stage from 2 on, and
the residual hypothesis (retired at 3) is held at 0.07 — the stage-3
wrist event is the only one the machinery can score.

## 8. Honest limits

- **The board cannot weigh the case's decisive evidence.** The
  dorsal-branch and forearm-motor patterns exclude the wrist
  anatomically and carry no LR, because no consulted source
  quantifies them. A reader who only follows the numbers sees the
  wrist alive until stage 3; the adjudication states the exclusion
  at stage 2 in prose, in anatomy. This is a property of the
  evidence base, not an oversight — inventing LRs for unquantified
  signs is the failure the module's discipline exists to prevent.
- **The Tinel LR+ is the weakest number in the case and is labelled
  as such.** Sensitivity from the primary study's own counts;
  specificity from a secondary evidence table; controls were
  healthy volunteers (spectrum bias inflates separation from a
  live differential). It is entered exactly as the cited pair
  computes it, caveat attached at every appearance, and the
  localization stands on three legs that do not use it (dorsal
  anatomy, forearm pattern, EDX localization).
- **The EDX LR is a floor over a heterogeneous range.** The
  parameter's 37–86% sensitivity spread spans 1983–1996 techniques
  and criteria; 7.4 is the least favourable combination, disclosed.
  A single modern criteria-validation pair exists in the literature
  (the 2017 Danish consensus-criteria validation) but validates a
  different criteria set than the protocol this case performs, so
  it was not substituted for the parameter the protocol follows.
- **Ontology granularity.** Cubital tunnel syndrome has its own
  Mondo class; Guyon's canal syndrome does not — the wrist
  hypothesis carries the generic ulnar neuropathy class
  (MONDO:0007006) with the gap stated, and the C8 hypothesis the
  generic radiculopathy class (case 4's precedent). No code was
  invented to make the differential look more coded than Mondo
  supports.
- **The deep branch has no record.** The intrinsic muscles'
  clinical records name the deep branch of the ulnar nerve as their
  innervation; the M35 pass leaves that phrase unresolved (no FMA
  class, no inventory record) — the exact position of case 4's
  recurrent branch of the median nerve. The intrinsic findings link
  the trunk records and say so.
- **Severity ≠ site, and treatment is not adjudicated.** No
  treatment guideline for cubital tunnel syndrome was consulted;
  the disposition is a referral with the localization and grade,
  and interim management is explicitly left to the receiving
  service rather than borrowed from unconsulted authority.
- **Governance, as every case:** synthetic patient, priors and
  test costs are verify-flagged placeholders, scores formative-only,
  expert-review gate **UNMET** — no reviewers are named because
  none exist. The native shell's GPU render stage cannot initialise
  on this VM (no Vulkan ICD); verification here is the data matrix
  of §9, as at Milestones 28–42.
